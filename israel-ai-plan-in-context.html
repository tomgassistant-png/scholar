Israel’s 300,000-GPU AI Plan in Context

What the 9 November 2026 targets mean for Israel’s grid and budget, and how they compare with the United States, the world, the largest company campuses and other countries

Prepared by Scholar · 11 November 2026

On 9 November 2026 the head of Israel’s National AI Directorate set a target of about 300,000 GPUs (AI computing chips) and about 2 gigawatts of electricity for AI. This report explains where those numbers came from, what already exists, what stands in the way, and how large the numbers are compared with Israel’s own power grid, with the United States and the world, with single company data centers, and with other countries’ programs, including per person.

Sources: Hebrew reporting and Israeli primary records come first (the Electricity Authority’s own draft decision, Calcalist, Globes, Walla, News1, People and Computers), then English sources. Each figure carries a link and a date. Tags: confirmed (a primary record or two independent reports), goal only (a stated target, not built), estimate (a modeled figure or my arithmetic, with assumptions stated), disputed (sources disagree), unsupported (no evidence found). Items I could not check in a second source are marked [unconfirmed]. Solid bars in the charts are things that exist; hatched bars are goals. Facts are as of 11 November 2026. Times and dates are Israel time.

The short answer: is it a lot?

For Israel, it is very large. The target of about 300,000 GPUs is roughly 30 to 75 times what is publicly documented as operating in the country today: about 4,000 GPUs in the Nebius cluster that hosts the national supercomputer, of which the state allocates 1,000, plus Nvidia’s own 2,048-GPU Israel-1 machine and a few thousand Blackwell chips in Nvidia’s private 30-megawatt center. [DatacenterDynamics, Jan 2026] [Israel Innovation Authority, 18 Jan 2026] [DatacenterDynamics, 23 Nov 2023] [Globes, 15 Jan 2025] The 2 gigawatts (GW) is about 8% of Israel’s whole installed generating capacity (about 24.6 GW in 2024), about 12% of the all-time peak (about 17 GW, August 2025), and about 22% of average demand (about 9 GW). [Mashak Energia, Mar 2026] [Calcalist, 20 Jul 2026]

Per person, it is a world-class goal. 300,000 GPUs for 10.3 million people is about 29,000 GPUs per million inhabitants, about six times South Korea’s 260,000-GPU deal per head, and far beyond any public national program in Europe or Asia. [Central Bureau of Statistics, Sep 2026] [NVIDIA Newsroom, 30 Oct 2025]

For the world, it is small. Epoch AI estimates the world’s stock of AI chips at about 31.6 million “H100-equivalents” in September 2026. Israel’s target is about 1% of that if counted one-for-one, or about 2% if the GPUs are of the newer Blackwell generation. It equals roughly one to two weeks of Nvidia’s sales at the mid-2026 rate. [Epoch AI, 2026] [Epoch AI, Oct 2026] The United States received about 72% of all AI compute delivered from 2022 to June 2026, about 19.8 million H100-equivalents; Israel’s target is about 1.5% to 3.5% of that. [Epoch AI, 2026]

Against single company sites, it is one campus. SpaceXAI’s Colossus 2 in Memphis alone was reported at about 1.19 million Blackwell GPUs in November 2026, four times Israel’s national target. [DatacenterDynamics, Nov 2026] OpenAI’s Stargate pledge is 10 GW, five times Israel’s 2 GW; Meta’s Hyperion plan is 5 GW. [XCyberNow, Oct 2026] [TechCrunch, 14 Jul 2025]

The hard limit is electricity, not ambition. In July 2026 Israel’s grid operator received requests to connect about 27 GW of data centers, more than the country’s entire generating capacity, and the regulator froze new requests. Its October 2026 draft would allow about 3.3 GW in total for data centers until 2034–2035, which would be roughly 20% of national electricity use, about the share Ireland has today (23% in 2025). [Electricity Authority, 18 Oct 2026] [Central Statistics Office, 7 Jul 2026]

No money is attached yet. The directorate’s 2026 budget is about NIS 3 billion (about $0.95 billion). Askal told the Knesset the plan needs about NIS 5 billion a year. The Times of Israel put the full plan at $2–5 billion a year, “none of that is committed so far.” The chips alone for 300,000 current-generation GPUs would cost roughly $12–40 billion (about NIS 38–128 billion). [Globes, 18 Aug 2026] [Israel Defense, 16 Aug 2026] [The Times of Israel, 9 Nov 2026]

1Part one

Background: the plan, the directorate and what exists

Sections 1–3

1. The announcement and the man behind it

On Monday 9 November 2026, at the AI and Cyber Week conference at Tel Aviv University, Brigadier General (reserves) Erez Askal, head of the National Artificial Intelligence Directorate (המטה הלאומי לבינה מלאכותית) in the Prime Minister’s Office, presented the main lines of Israel’s national strategy for artificial intelligence (AI). [Gadgety, 9 Nov 2026] [Calcalist Tech, 9 Nov 2026] [The Times of Israel, 9 Nov 2026] confirmed

Words used in this report

  • GPU (graphics processing unit): a chip first designed to draw images on screens, now the main type of chip used to train and run AI models. Nvidia makes most of them. Here “GPU” also covers similar AI chips (“accelerators”).
  • Watt, megawatt (MW), gigawatt (GW): units of electric power, the rate at which energy is used. 1 MW = 1,000 kilowatts; 1 GW = 1,000 MW. A gigawatt is about the output of one large power-station unit.
  • MVA (megavolt-ampere): the unit Israel’s regulator uses for the size of a grid connection. For large modern loads it is close to the same number of megawatts.
  • Data center (Hebrew: חוות שרתים, “server farm”): a building full of computers, with cooling and power equipment.
  • Foundation model: a large AI model trained on very broad data, which can then be adapted to many tasks. ChatGPT’s models are examples.
  • Sovereign AI: a state’s ability to control the AI models, chips, data and power that its important systems depend on, rather than relying fully on foreign companies.

What Askal said

He named three goals: technological sovereignty in AI, a better quality of life for citizens through AI, and using Israel’s strengths to advance AI worldwide. [The Times of Israel, 9 Nov 2026] confirmed He said that within five to seven years, power grids, water supply and hospitals will depend on AI, and: “If it’s not your AI, you have no control over your country.” [Calcalist Tech, 9 Nov 2026] [Ynetnews, 9 Nov 2026] confirmed

His physical targets were:

  • about 300,000 GPUs operating in Israel: about 100,000 for the defense establishment, universities, government ministries and startups, and about 200,000 to attract and grow commercial data centers [Gadgety, 9 Nov 2026] [Calcalist Tech, 9 Nov 2026] [Israel Defense, Nov 2026] goal only;
  • about 2 GW of electric power for AI infrastructure, data centers and chip plants [Gadgety, 9 Nov 2026] [The Times of Israel, 9 Nov 2026] goal only;
  • an Israeli foundation model, possibly built with partner countries: “we need to build our own frontier model, but we do not have to build it alone” [Calcalist Tech, 9 Nov 2026] goal only;
  • a National Institute for Impact-Driven AI (NIA), already set up, to apply AI in health, education and transport. [Gadgety, 9 Nov 2026] [Israel Defense, 16 Aug 2026] confirmed

Ynet reported that Askal “did not provide a budget or timetable” in the presentation. [Ynetnews, 9 Nov 2026] confirmed

Who Askal is

Askal commanded Unit 9900, the Israel Defense Forces’ visual and geographic intelligence unit, and later headed the IDF’s digital transformation administration in the C4I and Cyber Defense Directorate. He holds a law degree from Ono Academic College and a master’s degree in Middle Eastern and African studies from Tel Aviv University. [Calcalist, 12 Oct 2025] [People and Computers, 12 Oct 2025] confirmed Globes described him as a respected officer who received the Israel Defense Prize for satellite work, but with no high-tech or civilian management background and no science degree. [Globes, 18 Aug 2026] confirmed (The description is Globes’ assessment.)

When the directorate was created

The government approved the directorate on 25 September 2025 as a unit of the Prime Minister’s Office, with a budget of NIS 120 million for 2025–2026 (mostly for 2026), 20 staff positions, and NIS 13 million a year from 2027 for salaries and operations. [Calcalist, 19 Oct 2025] confirmed The Prime Minister’s Office announced Askal’s appointment on 12 October 2025. [Calcalist, 12 Oct 2025] [Israel Hayom, 12 Oct 2025] confirmed He was exempted from a public tender, on the ground of urgency and the security nature of the post, and was due to take up the post in early November 2025. [People and Computers, 12 Oct 2025] [Calcalist, 19 Oct 2025] confirmed Calcalist criticized the lack of a tender and the move of AI policy from the Ministry of Innovation, Science and Technology to the Prime Minister’s Office. [Calcalist, 19 Oct 2025] disputed

Why it matters

The 9 November speech is the first time a 300,000-GPU figure and a 2 GW figure have been stated together by the official in charge. They are targets in a speech. No government decision found so far contains the 300,000 figure.

The national supercomputer

The one large item from the TELEM program that was built is a national AI supercomputer. Nebius, an AI cloud company based in Amsterdam, won the Innovation Authority’s tender in May 2025. [DatacenterDynamics, Jan 2026] confirmed DCD reports the system at $140 million; Globes gives the state grant as NIS 160 million. [DatacenterDynamics, Jan 2026] [Globes, 18 Aug 2026] confirmed On 18 January 2026 the Innovation Authority announced that the system was operating and opened allocation of 1,000 Nvidia B200 GPUs, 70% to companies and 30% to academic research, at discounted prices. [Israel Innovation Authority, 18 Jan 2026] [The Jerusalem Post, Jan 2026] confirmed According to DCD, these 1,000 GPUs are a quarter of about 4,000 GPUs that Nebius deployed in an 8 MW space leased from the real-estate company Mega Or. [DatacenterDynamics, Jan 2026] confirmed

Nvidia’s own machines in Israel

  • Israel-1: Nvidia’s company supercomputer at Yokneam, 2,048 H100 GPUs, first phase operating from November 2023. It serves Nvidia’s research teams and “selected partners.” [DatacenterDynamics, 23 Nov 2023] confirmed
  • Mevo Carmel data center: a 30 MW Nvidia engineering facility near Yokneam with “several thousand” Blackwell GPUs, investment estimated at over $500 million, for Nvidia’s own use. [Globes, 15 Jan 2025] confirmed (The GPU number is an industry estimate reported by Globes.)
  • Kiryat Tivon campus: confirmed by Nvidia in December 2025. It is an office and research campus for up to 10,000 employees, not a data center. Construction is to start in 2027, with first occupancy planned for 2031. [Globes, 18 Dec 2025] confirmed

Private developers are also building. Nebius has further agreements for Mega Or’s sites in Modi’in and elsewhere, and Globes reports that the Finance Ministry hopes private firms such as Mega Or and SDS will buy “tens of thousands” of GPUs. [Globes, 18 Aug 2026] No official count of all GPUs operating in Israel was found. Adding the public figures above gives roughly ten thousand, which is a lower bound. [unconfirmed]

Why it matters

The baseline matters for judging the target. The state itself allocates 1,000 GPUs today. Even the 100,000 “domestic” part of the new target is about 100 times that, and 25 times the whole Nebius cluster.

3. The new plan and its money

Government Decision 4255 of 16 June 2026 set the strategy. The Electricity Authority quotes its key figure: Israel needs potential access, within the next five years, to computing power equal to 100,000 AI accelerators. [Electricity Authority, 18 Oct 2026] [Prime Minister’s Office, 16 Jun 2026] confirmed Israel Defense reports that the plan, presented by the directorate in August 2026, begins with an initial build of 5,000 accelerators, calls for a chip plant at 5 nanometers or below, and a study of computing in low Earth orbit. It adds that “the 100,000-accelerator target is not a government purchase,” and that the private market is to lead. [Israel Defense, 16 Aug 2026] confirmed

Globes, reporting on the same August press conference, described a “statement of intentions for the purchase of up to 100,000 Nvidia processors within a few years at an undisclosed cost,” and said the number was still in dispute: the Nagel committee asked for 60,000; the directorate wants 100,000; and the Accountant General and the directorate might settle for 3,000–6,000 more state-bought processors while private companies buy the rest. [Globes, 18 Aug 2026] disputed At that time the government planned to place the state supercomputer in a foreign country, according to Globes. [Globes, 18 Aug 2026] [unconfirmed]

The 9 November speech raised the headline number to 300,000 by adding about 200,000 GPUs for commercial data centers to the 100,000 domestic figure. [Gadgety, 9 Nov 2026] [Calcalist Tech, 9 Nov 2026] goal only

Why it matters

The money in hand (about NIS 3 billion for 2026, across many programs) would buy only a few thousand top GPUs. Most of the 300,000 would have to be bought by private companies, which depends on their being allowed to connect to the grid. That is why the electricity question in Part 2 decides the outcome.

Israel is an “electrical island”: it has no power links to neighboring grids, so it cannot import at times of peak demand. [Ilan Abramov, 27 Aug 2026] [unconfirmed]

Why it matters

Adding 2 GW of steady load would raise Israel’s average electricity demand by about a fifth. In the United States the same 2 GW would be about 10% of what data centers already draw.

5. The data-center freeze and the new quota

On 15 July 2026 the Electricity Authority set a temporary order (הוראת שעה) suspending, for 140 days, the grid operator Noga’s review of and replies to requests to connect data centers of 8 MVA or more. [Electricity Authority, 18 Oct 2026] confirmed The press reported it on 19–20 July 2026. [Calcalist, 20 Jul 2026] [News1, 20 Jul 2026] [People and Computers, Jul 2026] confirmed (Some reports give 8 megawatts; the Authority’s own documents use MVA.) The decision number of the July order was not found. [unconfirmed]

The reason: within about two months Noga had received requests for about 19,000 MVA, on top of an existing backlog of 8,000 MVA, for a total of about 27 GW. Noga warned the Authority and the Minister of Energy that it could not approve more without endangering grid stability, and that otherwise it would have to refuse more than 25,000 MW of requests. [Electricity Authority, 18 Oct 2026] [Walla! Finance, 20 Jul 2026] [Calcalist, 20 Jul 2026] confirmed The Authority said the total was higher than both the national peak and all generation and transmission capacity planned for the coming decades. [News1, 20 Jul 2026] confirmed

Much of the queue is probably speculative. Requests were cheap to file and a place in the queue was valuable; the Authority itself speaks of requests from parties “without commercial maturity” who have no contracts for AI chips or customers. [Electricity Authority, 18 Oct 2026] confirmed The largest single request on Noga’s list was about 1,111 MW at Hadera, and several requests of 500 MW each appeared, according to one analyst’s reading of the list. [Ilan Abramov, 27 Aug 2026] [unconfirmed]

Commitments already given

In July the Authority put existing commitments at about 1,500 MW, about 10% of national consumption early in the next decade. [Calcalist, 20 Jul 2026] [Walla! Finance, 20 Jul 2026] confirmed Its October draft revised this to about 2 GW: about 1.6 GW committed but not connected, and about 400 MVA actually connected, which would be about 14% of consumption. It added that these commitments use up all planned generating capacity until 2035, and are three to four times the computing power that the Nagel committee and the June 2026 national plan called for. [Electricity Authority, 18 Oct 2026] confirmed

The October 2026 draft decision

At meeting 752 on 18 October 2026 the Authority published for public comment a draft that would:

  • create a special quota of up to 1,000 MVA for new data centers, plus a reserve of up to 300 MVA for “local needs” (Globes reports that the 300 is meant for domestic use and the rest may serve customers abroad); [Electricity Authority, 18 Oct 2026] [Globes, Oct 2026] confirmed
  • cap each new data center at 200 MVA; [Electricity Authority, 18 Oct 2026] confirmed
  • require a bank guarantee of NIS 2 million per MVA requested, to screen out speculative requests, with the regulation period ending 1 June 2028; [Electricity Authority, 18 Oct 2026] confirmed
  • allow data centers to build their own generation on site. [Electricity Authority, 18 Oct 2026] confirmed

The Authority says this would bring data centers to about 20% of national electricity use within seven years, a share that “places Israel high in international comparison.” [Electricity Authority, 18 Oct 2026] confirmed Ireland, the usual extreme case, reached 23% in 2025. [Central Statistics Office, 7 Jul 2026] confirmed

When does the freeze end? The draft states that the suspension ends on 7 December 2026, but if no final decision is made by then, it continues until a further decision. [Electricity Authority, 18 Oct 2026] confirmed As of 11 November 2026 the rules are still a draft, not a final decision published in the official gazette. [Arnon, Oct 2026] confirmed The government separately adopted Decision 3586 of 4 December 2025 on preparing the electricity sector for AI demand. [Electricity Authority, 18 Oct 2026] confirmed

The Authority also gives its own cost yardstick: about $10 million per megawatt for an AI data center building and about $30 million per megawatt for the processors. On that basis it estimates over $100 billion of investment for the capacity it is allocating. [Electricity Authority, 18 Oct 2026] estimate

Why it matters

Askal’s 2 GW is about the size of the commitments the grid has already given. The regulator’s plan allows at most about 3.3 GW for all data centers until the mid-2030s, and only 0.4 GW is connected now. The 300,000-GPU target therefore competes for a fixed, small pool of power with every commercial data-center project in Israel.

One newer Blackwell B200 is roughly 2.3 H100e by this measure (my ratio of Nvidia’s published peak figures; estimate). Why it matters

On a world scale, Israel’s target is about the size of one large company campus, or one or two weeks of Nvidia’s output. It would not make Israel a major holder of AI computing power in absolute terms. It would make Israel one of the most computing-dense countries per person.

Per person

Dividing by population (rounded World Bank and national figures [World Bank, accessed 11 Nov 2026] [Central Bureau of Statistics, Sep 2026]): Israel’s target is about 29,000 GPUs per million people. South Korea’s deal is about 5,000 per million; Saudi Arabia’s Humain plan about 17,000 per million (but some of its sites are in the US); seven EU gigafactories would be about 1,600 per million. Installed national systems are far smaller: the UK’s Isambard-AI is about 79 per million, Japan’s ABCI 3.0 about 49, India’s pool about 26. For comparison, the US received about 58,000 H100e of AI compute per million people from 2022 to mid-2026 (19.8 million ÷ about 340 million). estimate So in H100-equivalent terms, Israel’s target of 300,000–690,000 H100e (about 29,000–67,000 per million) would put Israel close to the United States per person.

Why it matters

Israel’s target is small in absolute terms but very high per person, and it is mainly meant to attract commercial data centers (two-thirds of the total), not to serve only Israeli users. That makes it closer to the Gulf model of “compute as an export industry” than to the European research-computer model.

9. What 300,000 GPUs would draw

These are approximations. Each GPU needs power for itself, for its share of the server (processors, memory, network), and for the building (cooling, power conversion). The building overhead is expressed as PUE (power usage effectiveness): total facility power divided by computer power. A PUE of 1.2–1.3 is typical for new liquid-cooled AI halls (my assumption, estimate).

Review questions

Try each one first, then open it to check the answer.

1What share of Israel’s installed generating capacity is the 2 GW target, and why might average demand be a fairer comparison?
Answer. About 8% of roughly 24.6 GW (2024). Data centers run around the clock at a nearly steady load, so comparing with average demand (about 9 GW) shows their weight on the system better: 2 GW is about 22% of it.
2Why did the Electricity Authority freeze data-center connection requests in July 2026?
Answer. Requests had reached about 27 GW, more than the whole country’s generating capacity and about three times average demand. Noga said it could not approve more without endangering grid stability; the Authority suspended handling rather than reject almost all requests.
3How does Israel’s 300,000-GPU target compare with the world’s stock of AI computing power?
Answer. Epoch AI estimates about 31.6 million H100-equivalents worldwide in September 2026. 300,000 GPUs is about 1% if counted one-for-one, or about 2% if they are Blackwell-class chips worth about 2.3 H100-equivalents each.
4Why is the target described as large for Israel but small for the world?
Answer. It is 30–75 times the GPUs publicly documented in Israel and about 29,000 per million people, far above other national programs per head. But it is only about a quarter of one American campus (Colossus 2) and about one to two weeks of Nvidia’s sales.
5What is the difference between the 100,000 figure in Decision 4255 and the 300,000 figure in the speech?
Answer. The 100,000 is potential access, within five years, to computing equal to 100,000 accelerators for state, academic, defense and startup use. The 300,000 adds about 200,000 GPUs meant to attract commercial data centers. Only the 100,000 appears in a government decision found so far.
6Roughly how much power would 300,000 GPUs need?
Answer. About 0.5 GW for H100-class chips, about 0.65–0.7 GW for GB200-class, and about 0.95–1.15 GW for Vera Rubin-class, including cooling. So the GPUs alone need well under 2 GW.
7What would 300,000 GPUs cost, and how does that compare with the budget?
Answer. Roughly $12–16.5 billion for current GB300 chips and up to $32–40 billion for Vera Rubin, before buildings. The directorate’s 2026 budget is about NIS 3 billion (about $0.95 billion); Askal asked for NIS 5 billion a year. Most of the money would have to come from private investors.
8What does the Electricity Authority’s October 2026 draft propose, and what share of national electricity would data centers then use?
Answer. A quota of up to 1,000 MVA for new data centers plus 300 MVA for local needs, a cap of 200 MVA per site, and guarantees of NIS 2 million per MVA. With the roughly 2 GW already committed, data centers would use about 20% of national electricity within seven years, close to Ireland’s 23%.
